01 — Color

Color is assigned by job, not taste.

Orange does one thing: it means "act." Everything structural is steel/ink so the orange never competes with itself. Green, gold, and red are reserved — using them decoratively kills their signal.

03 — Space

Spacing is rhythm, not flat padding.

The old build padded every section the same (the "dashboard" tell). Here, proof and specs sit tight to feel dense and credible, while hero, offer, and CTA get loud breathing room — because whitespace around a decision point is what pulls the eye to it.

Section rhythm: --pad-tight (48px) for proof, specs, comparison, reviews · --pad-loud (88px) for hero, the offer block, and the final CTA. Two values, applied with intent — not one value everywhere.

04 — Action

One loud primary. Everything else defers.

Buttons are condensed uppercase — they read as commands. Orange primary is the only "buy" action on any view; ghost/inverse handle secondary paths. Button text matches the toast it produces (Add to Cart → Added).

Tap targets stay ≥44px tall for thumbs. Focus shows an orange ring for keyboard users. Never place two orange CTAs side by side — it splits the decision.

06 — Energy

The dark band — used once, on purpose.

One inverted block per page creates the athletic, high-contrast punch this lane is known for — best for the brand promise or final CTA. It's an intentional accent, not OS dark mode; the page stays light-locked.

07 — Discipline

How to keep it converting.

Do

  • One mega element per screen — usually the price or the promise.
  • Repeat rating + trust cues near every CTA.
  • Keep buttons, price, and badges identical from PDP to checkout.
  • Tight spacing for proof; loud spacing for decisions.
  • Left-align body; 16px minimum; Inter for anything you read.

Don't

  • Set paragraphs in the condensed display face.
  • Use gold or green decoratively — they signal ratings and trust.
  • Place two orange CTAs side by side.
  • Pad every section equally (the SaaS "dashboard" look).
  • Hardcode review counts or fake urgency — bind to real data.
